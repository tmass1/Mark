Say it with an arrow.

Mark is a fast, small screenshot tool for the Mac. Press ⌘4, drag across a region, point at what matters, and it’s on your clipboard.

Three keys, one screenshot.

  1. ⌘4

    Capture

    From anywhere on your Mac. Guides run the full width and height of the display while you aim, so an edge lines up with something on the far side of the screen. Adjust a corner afterwards, or type the exact size.

  2. drag

    Mark it up

    Arrows, lines, a pen, text, boxes, ellipses, a highlighter, a redaction and a crop. Colour and size come from the toolbar, and selecting something adopts its style, so the toolbar always says what the next edit will do.

  3. ⌘C

    Copy and close

    A PNG lands on the clipboard and the editor gets out of the way. ⌘S saves a file instead; ⇧⌘S hands it to Mail, Messages or AirDrop.

Small, and particular about it.

Arrows worth pointing with

Tapered, in the spirit of Skitch’s; solid, one width throughout; or thin, a stroke that doesn’t cover what it points at. The tip lands exactly where you let go.

Redact, don’t blur

A redaction replaces the region with coarse blocks averaged from the capture. A blur can be partly undone, and still gives away the shape of what’s under it.

Lives in the menu bar

No Dock icon, and no window until you want one. Open at Login keeps it ready, and the shortcut is yours to change.

Crop keeps your work

Everything you drew comes along, shifted to match, so cropping never quietly discards a drawing. ⌘Z puts the whole capture back.

Timed, for menus and hovers

Arm 3, 5 or 10 seconds. The screen clears so you can open what you’re after, and the countdown runs in the menu bar rather than over the shot.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No account, no cloud, no history on disk. Recent keeps the last six captures in memory, drawings and all — an undo for closing, not a library.

Real glass, in dark or light.

On macOS 26 the editor stands on Liquid Glass — the system’s own material, not a blurred rectangle. Dark is the default; light and Match System are a click away in Settings, along with the shortcut and Open at Login.
